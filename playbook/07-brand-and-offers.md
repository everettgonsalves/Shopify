# 07: Brand Name and Discount Code System

Researched Sep 28, 2026.

**Decisions:**
- **Name:** **SnootHug**. `snoothug.com` was available for $16/yr, checked through Shopify's domain registry lookup.
- **Discount system:** built to protect margin while growing reviews, your email list, and word of mouth. The launch code **FIRSTSNOOT** is already created and scheduled.

A reality check first: **a name won't make $1k/day. Your ads and your offer will.** The name has three smaller jobs:
1. **Be remembered and repeated.** When a gift recipient asks "where did you get this?", the giver has to be able to say the name.
2. **Look trustworthy** in ads and on card statements.
3. **Not get you into a trademark fight.**

## Part 1: The name

### How I judged names (what the evidence says)

| Principle | Evidence | What it means here |
|-----------|----------|--------------------|
| **Easy to say means more trusted** | Names that are easy to pronounce are rated more familiar, trustworthy, and valuable. Companies with easy-to-say names gained about 11.2% in their first week after IPO, versus about 4.4% for hard-to-say ones (Alter & Oppenheimer, 2006). | Must pass the "radio test": hear it once, spell it right. |
| **Repeated sounds feel good** | Brand names with sound repetition, spoken aloud, produced positive feelings that improved brand evaluations and choice across six experiments (Argo, Popa & Smith, *Journal of Marketing*, 2010). | A bonus, not a requirement. The tagline can carry it. |
| **Suggestive beats descriptive** | Suggestive, arbitrary, and made-up names can be registered as trademarks right away. Merely descriptive names ("Custom Pet Blankets") generally can't until they build recognition over time. | Avoid keyword names. Search traffic comes from product titles, not the store name. |
| **Speak the customer's language** | Pet-internet slang ("snoot," "boop," "toe beans," "zoomies") is in-group language that pet owners use themselves. BarkBox built its brand on in-jokes only dog people get. | Signals "made by pet people," and it fits TikTok. |
| **Don't look like the leaders** | Competitors cluster around "X & Paw" and portrait puns: Crown & Paw, Purr & Mutt, Paw & Glory, West & Willow, Crown & Canvas, Iconic Paw, Pop Your Pup. | Avoid those patterns so your ads don't blur into theirs. |

### Why SnootHug wins

- **Instant meaning.** "Their snoot" is your pet. "Hug" is something you hold. Cold ad traffic gets it in two syllables.
- **In-group language.** It's pet-people slang, and it covers **dogs and cats**, since both have snoots.
- **Passes the radio test.** It's spelled exactly how it sounds, 8 letters, and fits on a logo, a gift tag, or a card statement.
- **Covers every angle:**
  - Gift: "Give them a SnootHug this Christmas."
  - Self-purchase: "Hug their face every day."
  - Memorial: "One more SnootHug."
- **Likely registrable,** because it's suggestive rather than descriptive. Confirm with the search below.
- **Weakness:** no repeated sound. The taglines make up for it.

### Taglines

- **Main:** *Their face. Your hug.*
- **Ad hook:** *Hug their face every day.*
- **Memorial:** *One more snoot hug.*
- **Gift:** *The gift every pet parent hugs back.*

### Brand setup checklist

- [ ] **Buy `snoothug.com` in your existing store:** Settings → Domains → Buy new domain. Don't use a signup link; that creates a new store. Optionally also buy `snoothugs.com` (DNS showed it unregistered) and point it at the main domain for people who add an "s."
- [ ] **Rename the store:** Settings → Store details → Store name → `SnootHug`.
- [ ] **Support email** `help@snoothug.com`. Domains bought through Shopify include email forwarding.
- [ ] **Card statement descriptor:** `SNOOTHUG` (Settings → Payments).
- [ ] **Social handles:** claim `@snoothug` on TikTok, Instagram, Facebook, and Pinterest today, even before you post. Fallbacks: `@snoothugco` or `@shopsnoothug`.
- [ ] **Trademark check:**
  - Search [USPTO Trademark Search](https://tmsearch.uspto.gov) for `SNOOTHUG`, `SNOOT HUG`, and `SNOOT`.
  - Classes to check: 24 (blankets), 16 (prints/posters), 28 (Christmas ornaments), 35 (online retail).
  - A trademark attorney's quick conflict check is cheap insurance before you spend on ads.
  - Filing under the 2025 USPTO fee schedule costs about $350 per class. That can wait until the product proves itself.
- [ ] **Regenerate the Privacy policy** after the rename. The current one says "My Store" and shows your personal Gmail.

## Part 2: The discount code system

### The rule that drives everything

**Every 10% off costs about $8 per order.** That lowers your break-even cost per purchase from about $37 to about $29 (see file 02). So a discount must *buy* something specific: an email address, a first review, a creator's audience, or a holiday sale. Never discount "just because."

**What the coupon data says:**
- 24% of shoppers only go looking for a promo code when they **see an empty code field** at checkout.
- About 35% of promo codes fail at checkout, and **24% of shoppers abandon when a code doesn't work**.

Two consequences:
1. Offers you advertise should be **automatic** (no code needed).
2. Public codes should be **capped or unique** so they don't leak to coupon sites and browser extensions.

### Your discount system

| Offer | Purpose | Settings | Status |
|-------|---------|----------|--------|
| **FIRSTSNOOT** | Seed the first real orders, reviews, and customer photos. Social proof is what lowers your ad costs later. | **20% off everything. 50 uses total, one per customer, doesn't combine with other discounts. Runs Oct 8 12:00 AM PT through Oct 31 11:59 PM PT.** | ✅ **Created and scheduled** |
| **Welcome 10%** (email/SMS signup) | Grow the list that should drive 20–30% of revenue | **Unique single-use codes** from Klaviyo: prefix `WELCOME`, 10% off, expires 7 days after it's sent | ⏳ After you install Klaviyo |
| **Creator codes** (e.g. `MILO15`) | The popularity engine. The creator's followers get a reason to buy now, and each code tracks exactly which creator drove the sale. | 15% off for followers, plus about 10% commission. That still leaves about $17 per order on unpaid creator sales. | ⏳ I'll create one per creator as you sign them |
| **Black Friday / Cyber Monday** | The biggest sales week | **Automatic, no code:** a free ornament with any blanket, *or* the blanket + ornament bundle at $89.95. Early access Nov 25 for your email list, public Nov 26–30. | ⏳ I'll create it once products exist |
| **Abandoned checkout, email 3 only** | Win back people who are on the fence | Unique code from Klaviyo: 10% off or a free ornament | ⏳ After Klaviyo |
| **Referral** ("Give $10, get $10") | Word of mouth from gift buyers | Add after the first ~50 orders, using a referral app | Later |

### How to share FIRSTSNOOT (and stay on the right side of the FTC)

- **Where:**
  - your TikTok and Instagram bio and posts ("First 50 orders: 20% off with FIRSTSNOOT")
  - local Facebook and Nextdoor pet groups (follow each group's rules)
  - dog parks, groomers, and vets (a small QR flyer)
  - your own network
- **Ask every buyer for an honest review with a photo**, and separately for permission to share the photo. **Never make the discount depend on a positive review.**
- **Friends and family can buy and post photos.** Under the FTC's review rule, don't present close friends' or relatives' reviews as independent customer reviews unless the connection is disclosed.

### Discount rules

1. No discount that runs all the time sitewide, and no fake "compare at" prices.
2. Codes don't stack. Every discount is set to not combine with others.
3. Every code has an end date or a usage cap.
4. Offers shown in ads are automatic, so shoppers aren't sent hunting for a code.
5. Every Sunday, check Analytics → Reports for discount usage and average order value. If a code shows up on a coupon site, end it and replace it.
